# The content operating model (how companies actually do it)

Generalisable beyond video. This is a stage-gate development system: the same shape studios, game companies and product orgs use. Swap "video" for "feature" or "product" and it still holds.

## The core principle

**A company's advantage is not better execution. It is that nothing expensive happens before it has been earned.**

An amateur makes the thing, publishes it, then hopes. A company kills 90% of ideas cheaply, at a stage where killing costs nothing. By the time real work starts, the idea has already survived several rounds of vetting.

From studio practice: *"A greenlight decision rarely happens in a single dramatic moment and is the culmination of weeks or months of development, during which a project passes through multiple internal gates."* And: *"By the time a project reaches the formal greenlight stage, it has already survived several rounds of informal vetting."*

A greenlight proposal must make **both a creative case and an execution case.** Either alone is a no.

## The pipeline (7 stages, 3 gates)

```
1. IDEA BANK        cheap, high volume, zero judgment
        │
   [GATE 1: SCORING]  ─── fail ──> back to bank
        │
2. PACKAGING        title + thumbnail, BEFORE the script
        │
   [GATE 2: PACKAGING TEST] ─── fail ──> back to bank
        │
3. SCRIPT
4. PRODUCTION PLAN
5. EDIT
6. PUBLISH
        │
   [GATE 3: REVIEW]  ─── feeds back into the bank
```

**The two places every channel stalls** (and the reason most never grow):
1. An idea bank with **no scoring gate**.
2. **Packaging done after the edit instead of before the script.**

Both are the same mistake: doing expensive work before cheap work has earned it.

## Gate 1 — Idea scoring

Score every idea 1–5 on four axes:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| Demand proof | Is there evidence viewers already want this? |
| Viewer pain | Does it solve a real viewer problem or itch? |
| Channel fit | Does it fit the channel promise and pillars? |
| Original angle | Do we have a fresh angle, or is it a rerun? |

Outcome is one of five: produce, refine, test, postpone, kill. Written down, not felt.

## Gate 2 — Packaging is the greenlight

MrBeast's production document: **the creative process for every video starts with the title and thumbnail.** They set viewer expectations, and everything downstream is defined to match them.

The example from the document:
- "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" — boring
- "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" — viral

**Identical production cost. Completely different outcome.** You cannot edit your way out of a weak idea, and the title is where idea quality becomes visible before any money is spent.

Rule: *"An idea that fails packaging goes back to the bank rather than forward to production."*

## The metrics that are allowed to matter

MrBeast's team is held to exactly three:

- **CTR** — did the packaging earn the click?
- **AVD** (average view duration) — absolute time watched
- **AVP** (average view percentage) — proportion watched

Everything else (subscribers, likes, comments, follower count) is downstream noise at the decision stage.

## The first minute

*"The first minute is where retention is either won or lost."*

The directive: front-load information while matching the expectation the thumbnail and title set.

Notably, the document accepts **losing 21 million of 60 million viewers in minute one** as fine — provided the promise was kept. The goal is not retaining everyone. It is retaining the people the packaging actually promised something to.

## Gate 3 — The review loop

Once a video passes ~500 views:

1. Open the retention graph.
2. Mark every timestamp where the curve drops **more than 5 percentage points in under 20 seconds.**
3. Scrub to that exact moment, watch 30 seconds either side, and name the cause.
4. Three dip types to classify: **the opening cliff, the mid-roll valley, the end-of-content drop.**
5. An early drop greater than 40% means the hook failed to deliver immediate value.
6. Look for **repeated patterns across multiple uploads**, not one-offs.
7. **Change exactly one thing before the next upload.** One variable, or you learn nothing.

Creators who review retention at least 3x/month report **19% higher retention.**

## People and leverage

- **A-players**: *"obsessive, learn from mistakes, coachable, intelligent, don't make excuses."* The document's test is learning rate, not current skill.
- C-players are described as *"poisonous"* and moved out fast.
- **Consultants are "cheat codes"**: rather than reinventing, call the person who already solved the exact problem. *"Call the person who made the previous world's largest slice of cake."*
- Obsess over **"critical components"** — the few elements the thing lives or dies on get daily attention and a backup plan. Everything else is allowed to be average.

## What does NOT transfer from MrBeast

Honest limits. His model assumes huge budgets, large crews and physical spectacle. Those parts are irrelevant to a solo faceless operation.

**Transfers:** packaging-first, idea quality over execution quality, three-metric discipline, the first-minute obsession, consultants as shortcuts, critical-components focus, the review loop.

**Does not transfer:** $500k prizes, 100-person crews, location scouting, "wow factor" spectacle as the hook.

## The generalisable takeaway

Cheap work gates expensive work. Score before you package, package before you script, script before you produce, review before you repeat. The gate is always the cheapest test that can kill the idea.

This is the part worth reusing for any product, not just video.
