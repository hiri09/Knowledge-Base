# Audience psychology (universal)

How human attention actually works. Platform-agnostic — every rule in `platforms/` is a local application of something on this page.

## 1. Dopamine is prediction error, not pleasure

Midbrain dopamine neurons fire on **reward-prediction error** — the gap between what the viewer expected and what arrived. Not on the reward itself.

**Consequence:** content that delivers exactly what was expected produces no response at all. The feeling you are engineering is *"that is not what I thought was coming."*

Feeds exploit this by mixing forgettable clips with occasional standouts — the unpredictability is the mechanism. A predictable video is not a safe video; it is an invisible one.

**Apply it:** every promise should resolve slightly off from where the viewer predicted. Right answer, unexpected shape.

## 2. The curiosity gap is physical discomfort

Loewenstein's Information Gap Theory: curiosity is *"a mental itch."* The brain treats unresolved information as genuinely uncomfortable and will keep watching to close the gap.

**Consequence:** you do not hold attention by being interesting. You hold it by **opening a loop and refusing to close it yet.**

- Open a loop in the first seconds.
- Never close one without opening the next.
- Close every loop you opened before the end. An unclosed loop at the end reads as a cheat and kills trust.

## 3. Attention resets on change, every 3–5 seconds

*"Human attention evolved to prioritize change."* A **pattern interrupt** is a sudden shift in visuals, audio or pacing that resets focus.

The working cadence: **a state change every 3–5 seconds, and a small payoff every 3–5 seconds, so attention never has a reason to drop.**

State change = cut, zoom, new graphic, music shift, tone shift, a number appearing on screen. It does not have to be loud. It has to be *different from the previous 3 seconds*.

## 4. The eye goes to faces, then emotion, then colour

- The brain prioritises **faces and body posture** above everything else in a frame.
- Within a face, attention goes to the **eyes** first — the fixation pattern is eyes → nose → mouth, with most resources on the eyes.
- Attention is biased toward the **emotion-carrying regions** of a face even when other regions are more visually salient.
- **Negative emotion is detected faster than positive.** Shock, fear and confusion out-pull delight.
- **Colour wins by contrast, not by hue.** A coloured item among muted items captures attention; saturation, brightness and fidelity all measurably shift where people look.

**Apply it:** a face with a readable negative emotion beats an object. One saturated element against a desaturated frame beats a colourful frame. A frame where everything is loud has nothing loud in it.

## 5. Stories bypass evaluation

**Narrative transportation:** when someone is absorbed in a story they lose awareness of their surroundings and become emotionally invested in the outcome. Listener brain activity becomes *coupled* to the storyteller's.

Transported viewers **counter-argue less** and are measurably more likely to act afterwards. Both transportation and concern for the characters predict post-story behaviour.

**Consequence:** facts delivered as information get evaluated. The same facts delivered as a story get absorbed. For a business/documentary channel this is the entire game — "a company lost $200M" is data; "the three people who knew and were ignored" is a story.

**Apply it:** every piece needs a character, a want, an obstacle and a cost. A number with no one standing behind it is forgettable.

## 6. Suspense is the delivery vehicle

*"Narratives that cause us to pay attention and also involve us emotionally are the stories that move us to action — this is what a good documentary film does."*

The structure that holds: rising tension + character development, together. Tension without a character is anxiety. A character without tension is a biography.
