# Attention and retention mechanics (universal)

The numbers. These are measurement thresholds, not opinions.

## The opening

- **50–60% of all drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds.**
- **3-second retention is the hook metric: below 45% the hook is broken. Above 55% it works.** Between the two, it is unproven.
- Moving retention from 30% → 55% can increase reach **10–50x**. Reach is a step function, not a slope — this is why marginal hook improvements produce non-marginal results.

## The first minute (long-form)

*"The first minute is where retention is either won or lost."*

The directive: **front-load information while matching the expectation the packaging set.**

Losing most of the opening audience in minute one is acceptable **provided the promise was kept**. The goal is not to retain everyone. It is to retain the people the packaging actually promised something to. A hook that retains the wrong audience is worse than one that sheds them fast.

## The seed test

Every short-form piece is shown to a small seed audience first. Weak engagement there and distribution **stops** — there is no recovery, no second chance, no late bloom. The piece is decided in its first hours.

This is why volume matters more than polish early: you are buying test cycles, not making artifacts.

## Reading a retention graph (after ~500 views)

1. Mark every timestamp where the curve drops **more than 5 percentage points in under 20 seconds.**
2. Scrub to that exact moment. Watch 30 seconds either side. **Name the cause in words.**
3. Classify the dip: **opening cliff / mid-roll valley / end-of-content drop.**
4. An early drop **greater than 40%** means the hook did not deliver immediate value, or the intro took too long to reach the promise.
5. Look for **patterns repeated across multiple uploads.** A single video's dip is noise.
6. **Change exactly one thing before the next upload.**

Creators who review retention at least 3x/month report **19% higher retention.**

## The three metrics

| Metric | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **CTR** | Did the packaging earn the click? |
| **AVD** (average view duration) | How much absolute time did we hold? |
| **AVP** (average view percentage) | What proportion did we hold? |

Diagnostic pairing:
- Low CTR, high AVP → the content is good and nobody is seeing it. **Fix packaging.**
- High CTR, low AVP → the packaging over-promised. **Fix the opening, or stop lying in the title.**
- Low, low → the idea failed Gate 1. Do not fix it. Kill it.

## Length

- Short-form: **50–60 seconds has the best watch-through (~76%)**, even where 3 minutes is permitted. Longer is not better; completed is better.
- Business documentary long-form: **12–17 minutes** is the established working range for the format.
