# Bonds and fixed income

A bond is a **loan you own**. You lend ₹1,000, receive a fixed coupon each year, and
get the ₹1,000 back at maturity. Equity = ownership + unlimited upside + last in
line. Debt = a contract + capped upside + first in line.

## The one thing to internalise: price moves opposite to rates

You hold a bond paying 7% (₹70/yr on ₹1,000). New bonds start paying 9%. Nobody will
buy your 7% bond at ₹1,000 when ₹1,000 buys ₹90/yr elsewhere. Your bond's price must
fall until its yield matches — roughly ₹778. **Rates up → bond prices down.**

**Duration** measures how much: a 10-year bond falls far more than a 1-year bond for
the same rate move, because more future payments get repriced.

## Why an equity investor cares

The government bond yield is the **risk-free alternative** and the discount rate for
every stock. When the 10-year goes from 6% to 8%, every valuation in the market is
mechanically worth less — no company did anything wrong. This is the single biggest
driver of "the whole market fell and nothing happened".

## The main risks

- **Interest-rate risk** — the above.
- **Credit risk** — the borrower doesn't pay. A 12% corporate bond is offering you
  5% extra for a reason; read the reason.
- **Inflation risk** — 7% fixed is a loss in real terms at 8% inflation.

## How I'd get this wrong

Treating a high-yield debt fund as a safe FD substitute. Extra yield is always
payment for risk. There is no exception, and the risk shows up all at once.
