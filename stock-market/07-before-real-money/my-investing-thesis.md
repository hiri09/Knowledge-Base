# My investing thesis (fill this in before the first rupee)

This is the file that makes everything else enforceable. Written in advance, in his
own words, and edited only on purpose — never in the middle of a drawdown.

## 1. Money and horizon

- Amount I can invest: ₹____ (money I will not need for ___ years)
- Emergency fund (6 months expenses, in cash/liquid) in place? ___
- High-interest debt cleared? ___
- Monthly amount I can add: ₹____

**Nothing goes into equity until the first two are yes.** Equity's only real edge is
time, and the only way to lose that edge is to be forced to sell early.

## 2. What drawdown I can actually sit through

Not what sounds brave — what I'd hold through without selling.
"If this portfolio is down ___% for ___ months, I will: ____________."

## 3. What I will and won't buy

- Will: ___ (e.g. index funds; profitable businesses I can explain in two sentences)
- Won't: ___ (e.g. F&O, penny stocks, IPO day buys, anything from a tip)
- Max per single stock: ___% of portfolio
- Max per sector: ___% of portfolio
- Risk per idea: ___% of capital (see `../05-not-losing-money/risk-and-position-sizing.md`)

## 4. My checklist before any buy

1. What does this business sell, and who pays for it?
2. Why will it earn more in 5 years than today?
3. What's the moat, and is ROCE showing it?
4. Does operating cash flow track profit over 5 years?
5. What price am I paying for that, vs. its own history and peers?
6. **What would prove me wrong, and at what price do I admit it?**
7. Why is it available at this price — what does the seller believe?

If I can't answer 1, 2 and 6, I don't buy. No exceptions, no "small position just to
track it".

## 5. Review cadence

Portfolio review: once a quarter, with results. Not daily. Checking daily converts
an investment into a slot machine.

## How I'd get this wrong

Writing this and never reading it again. It gets read before every buy, and the
checklist answers go into `paper-trading-log.md` (same folder) where they can be scored later.
