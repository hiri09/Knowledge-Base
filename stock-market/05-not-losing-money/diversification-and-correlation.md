# Diversification and correlation

Diversification is the one free lunch: lower risk at the same expected return. But
only if the things you hold **don't fall for the same reason**.

## Correlation is the whole point

Ten stocks sounds diversified. Ten PSU banks is **one** bet on credit growth and
rates wearing ten names. They'll drop together on the same morning.

Diversify across the thing that can go wrong:
- **Sector** — IT, banking, FMCG, pharma, energy respond to different forces.
- **Factor** — rate-sensitive vs. commodity-linked vs. domestic-consumption.
- **Geography / currency** — IT earns in dollars, so a falling rupee helps it and
  hurts an importer.
- **Asset class** — equity, debt, gold, cash. Correlations between them stay low in
  normal times (and converge toward 1 in a true panic — plan for that).

## Simple example

Portfolio A: TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, Tech Mahindra. One bad US IT-spending quarter
takes all five down 12% together.
Portfolio B: TCS, HDFC Bank, Hindustan Unilever, Sun Pharma, an index fund. Same bad
quarter costs you maybe 3%.

## How many is enough

Roughly 12-20 genuinely different names captures most of the benefit. Beyond ~25
you've built an expensive index fund by hand and can no longer follow any of them.
Fewer than 5 and one accounting fraud ends your year.

## How I'd get this wrong

"Diversifying" into five more stocks I like, which are all mid-cap, all domestic, all
rate-sensitive, bought in the same bullish month. That's concentration with extra
steps.
