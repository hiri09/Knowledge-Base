# Behavioural traps — the ones that will actually cost you money

Your brain is well built for the savannah and badly built for markets. Name these so
you can catch yourself mid-act.

**Loss aversion** — a ₹10,000 loss hurts about twice as much as a ₹10,000 gain feels
good. Result: cutting winners early, holding losers forever ("it's only a loss when
I sell" — no, it's a loss now; selling only changes who holds the risk).

**Anchoring** — fixating on your buy price, or the 52-week high. The market doesn't
know what you paid. The only question is: *at today's price, would I buy it today?*
If no, you're holding out of embarrassment, not analysis.

**FOMO / herding** — the strongest one. A stock is up 60% in two months, everyone is
posting about it, and the risk feels *lowest* exactly when it is highest.

**Recency bias** — three good months feel like a permanent regime. Bull markets make
everyone feel skilled; leverage arrives right before the correction.

**Confirmation bias** — reading only the bullish thread about the stock you own.
Antidote: write down, before buying, what would make you sell.

**Overconfidence / overtrading** — more trades feel like more control. They are more
fees and more chances to be wrong.

**Sunk cost + revenge trading** — doubling down to "get back" what the market took.
The market has no memory of your loss and no obligation to return it.

## Simple example

Bought at ₹300, now ₹210. "I'll sell when it gets back to ₹300." That sentence
contains no information about the business — only about the anchor. Meanwhile the
₹2,10,000 sitting in that position is capital that can't be in a better idea.

## The one defence that works

**Write the thesis and the exit before buying.** Decisions made in advance, in
writing, are the only ones that survive contact with a red screen. That's what
`paper-trading-log.md` is for.

## How I'd get this wrong

Reading this list, agreeing with all of it, and then doing every single one anyway —
because knowing a bias exists doesn't disable it. Only a written rule does.
