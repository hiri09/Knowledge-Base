# What actually moves a price

In the moment: **only order flow**. Price rises because buyers are willing to lift
the ask; it falls because sellers hit the bid. Everything else is a reason people
*decide* to do that.

The reasons, roughly in order of durability:

1. **Earnings and cash flow** — more profit per share, over years. The only force
   that compounds.
2. **Expectations vs. reality** — a stock falls on great results because the market
   priced in *greater*. Price reflects the forecast, so what matters is the surprise.
3. **Interest rates** — higher rates make safe bonds attractive and make far-future
   profits worth less today. Rates up → valuations compress, growth stocks hurt most.
4. **Flows** — index inclusion, SIP money, FII buying/selling. Mechanical demand,
   indifferent to value.
5. **Narrative and sentiment** — strongest short term, weakest long term.

## Simple example

Infosys reports profit up 12%. Analysts expected 18%. Stock drops 6% on a good
quarter. Nothing is broken — the *expectation* was repriced, not the business.

## The mental model

**Price = earnings × what people will pay for ₹1 of those earnings (the multiple).**
Earnings move slowly and are knowable. The multiple moves fast and is a mood. Most
one-year returns are mood; most ten-year returns are earnings.

## How I'd get this wrong

Reading the day's news as the cause of the day's move. The headline is written after
the move, to explain it. Causation is assigned, not observed.
