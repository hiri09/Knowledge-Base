# Who is on the other side of your trade

Every trade has someone taking the opposite view (or no view at all). Knowing which,
tells you how much to read into a move.

- **Retail (you)** — small size, slow information, emotional. Collectively huge now
  via SIPs.
- **DII** — Indian mutual funds, insurers, pension money. Steady, rule-bound,
  relatively price-insensitive (SIP money must be deployed monthly).
- **FII/FPI** — foreign funds. Big, fast, and reacting to global rates and the rupee
  as much as to the company. The usual source of sharp index-level swings.
- **Promoters** — the founding owners. Their buying/selling is the most informed
  signal available and is publicly disclosed.
- **Market makers / HFT** — provide the quotes, earn the spread, have no opinion.
  Most of your "instant fill" comes from them.
- **Operators** — in illiquid small-caps, coordinated players who manufacture volume
  and price. Where "the stock that only goes up" lives, until it doesn't.

## Simple example

A stock jumps 4% on no news. If it's a ₹2 lakh crore company, that's real repricing
by large money. If it's a ₹200 crore company on ₹30 lakh of volume, one buyer did
that — and one seller can undo it.

## How I'd get this wrong

Assuming the other side is dumber than me. On the other side of my "obvious" trade is
often someone who has read the annual report, knows the sector, and is paid full-time
to do this. My edge has to be patience and horizon, not speed or information.
