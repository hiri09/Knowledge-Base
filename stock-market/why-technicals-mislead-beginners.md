# Why technicals mislead beginners

Technical analysis isn't fake — order flow and crowd behaviour are real. The traps
are in how a beginner uses it.

**1. Hindsight is a perfect teacher of nothing.** Every chart in every tutorial is
already resolved. Scroll right and the pattern "worked". Live, you're on the left
edge, where the same shape precedes both outcomes.

**2. Patterns are found, not predicted.** The brain is a pattern engine; random data
produces head-and-shoulders all day. Flip a coin 200 times, plot it, and you'll see
trends, support, and clean breakouts.

**3. Indicators are derivatives of price.** RSI, MACD, Bollinger — all computed from
the same OHLC. Six indicators is one piece of information wearing six hats, and the
agreement feels like confirmation.

**4. The timeframe sets the noise floor.** Shorter chart = more noise, more signals,
more trades, more fees, worse outcomes. Costs scale with activity; edge doesn't.

**5. Self-fulfilling until crowded.** Round numbers and the 200-DMA matter *because*
everyone watches them — and stop hunts happen for the same reason.

## Simple example

Backtest "buy when RSI < 30" on 10 years of Nifty and it looks profitable. Add 0.1%
cost per round trip and 0.05% slippage, and the edge is gone. The strategy didn't
fail — it never cleared its own costs.

## The usable part

Use charts for **timing and risk, not for deciding what to own**:
where the thesis is proven wrong (stop level), whether the market agrees yet
(trend, volume), and how much room to the next obstacle (sizing).

## How I'd get this wrong

Letting the chart pick the stock. The chart has no idea what the company sells.
