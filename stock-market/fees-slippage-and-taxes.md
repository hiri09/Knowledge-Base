# Fees, slippage and taxes — the silent drag

Every round trip costs you, win or lose. Small per trade, decisive in aggregate.

## What's actually charged (India, equity delivery vs. intraday)

- **Brokerage** — ₹0-20 per order (delivery often free, intraday ~₹20 or 0.03%).
- **STT** — 0.1% on both sides for delivery; 0.025% on the sell side for intraday.
- **Exchange + SEBI charges, stamp duty, GST** — small, but on every order.
- **Slippage** — the gap between the price you saw and the price you got. Invisible
  on the contract note, often the biggest cost of all.
- **Bid-ask spread** — you buy at the ask and sell at the bid, so you start every
  trade slightly underwater.

## Simple example

₹1,00,000 intraday round trip at ~0.05% all-in each way + 0.05% slippage ≈ **₹200**.
Nothing. Do it once a trading day for a year: 250 × ₹200 = **₹50,000 — 50% of a
₹1,00,000 account.** You now need a 50% gross return to break even.

## Taxes (equity, current regime)

- **Short-term capital gains** (held ≤12 months): 20%.
- **Long-term capital gains** (>12 months): 12.5%, with ~₹1.25 lakh/year exempt.
- **Dividends**: taxed at your slab rate.
- Losses can be set off and carried forward 8 years — only if you file returns on
  time.

Holding past the 12-month line is a real, quantifiable return bump. Churning is
taxed at nearly double the rate *and* costs fees.

## How I'd get this wrong

Judging a strategy on gross returns. The only number that exists is net of fees,
slippage, and tax. A 15% gross strategy traded weekly can net less than an index fund
held and ignored.
