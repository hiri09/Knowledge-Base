# Risk and position sizing — the only part that decides survival

You don't control returns. You control **how much you lose when you're wrong**, and
how often you're exposed. That's the whole job.

## The asymmetry nobody feels until it happens

| Loss | Gain needed to recover |
|---|---|
| −10% | +11% |
| −25% | +33% |
| −50% | +100% |
| −75% | +300% |

Losses compound against you harder than gains compound for you. Avoiding the −50% is
worth more than catching any single winner.

## The 1% rule (one line of arithmetic)

Risk at most **1% of capital per idea** — not 1% position size, 1% *loss* if the
stop is hit.

```
Quantity = (capital × 1%) ÷ (entry price − stop price)
```

**Example.** Capital ₹5,00,000. Risk budget ₹5,000. Buy at ₹200, thesis is wrong
below ₹180 → risk per share ₹20 → **250 shares** (₹50,000 position, 10% of capital).
If the stop is tighter — wrong below ₹190 — risk ₹10/share → 500 shares (₹1,00,000).
**The stop decides the size. Never the other way round.**

Twenty consecutive losses at 1% leaves you with 82% of capital. Five losses at 20%
leaves you with 33%.

## Where the stop goes

At the price that **proves the reason for buying was wrong** — below the structure,
the support, the level. Never at a round number of rupees you're emotionally willing
to lose; the market has never heard of your pain threshold.

## How I'd get this wrong

Sizing by conviction: "I'm very sure, so I'll put 40% in." Conviction is the feeling
you have right before you find out what you didn't know. Size by the stop, every
time.
