# Indices — Nifty 50, Sensex, and what they hide

An index is a **basket of stocks reduced to a single number**, so you can say "the
market" in one word. Nifty 50 = 50 large NSE companies. Sensex = 30 large BSE
companies.

Indian indices are **free-float market-cap weighted**: bigger company, publicly
tradable portion, bigger influence.

## Simple example

A 3-stock index: A (₹10,000 cr), B (₹5,000 cr), C (₹1,000 cr).
Weights: A 62.5%, B 31.25%, C 6.25%.
A rises 10%, C falls 20% → index moves +6.25% − 1.25% = **+5%**.
So "the market is up 5%" even though a third of its members crashed.

## What it's good for

- A benchmark: if your picks return 11% and the Nifty returned 15%, you lost to a
  fund you could have bought in one click.
- A cheap, diversified default — an index fund buys all 50 in those weights.

## What it hides

- Breadth. The index can rise while most stocks fall (a handful of heavyweights
  carry it).
- Survivorship. Losers get dropped and replaced by winners, so the index quietly
  upgrades itself. Your portfolio doesn't do that on its own.

## How I'd get this wrong

Using the index to judge my stock daily. A small-cap and the Nifty answer to
different forces; "Nifty is green, why is my stock red" is a question with no useful
answer.
