# Reading a chart (the honest minimum)

A chart is a picture of **who was willing to transact, at what price, when**. That's
all it is. Everything below is a vocabulary for describing that picture.

## The candle

One candle = one period (1 minute, 1 day…). It carries four numbers:
**Open, High, Low, Close (OHLC)**.
Body = open→close. Wick = the extremes it reached but didn't hold.
Green/up: close > open. Red/down: close < open.

A long upper wick means buyers pushed up and got rejected. A big body on big volume
means one side dominated the whole period.

## The four things worth knowing

1. **Trend** — higher highs + higher lows (up), lower highs + lower lows (down),
   neither (range). Most of the time it's a range.
2. **Support / resistance** — price levels where it repeatedly stopped. Not magic:
   it's memory. People who bought there defend it; people trapped there sell at
   break-even.
3. **Volume** — conviction. A breakout on low volume is a rumour; on 3x average
   volume it's a decision.
4. **Moving average** — the average close of the last N periods, redrawn each bar.
   A 50-day MA is just "what the last 50 days paid on average". Price above = recent
   buyers are in profit.

## Simple example

Stock stalls at ₹240 three times over two months, then closes at ₹252 on 4x volume.
Reading: the sellers sitting at ₹240 are gone, and it took real money to clear them.
That's a breakout. If it drifts back under ₹240 within days on fading volume, the
reading was wrong — which is exactly where the stop-loss belongs.

## How I'd get this wrong

Finding patterns in noise. On a 5-minute chart everything looks like a signal. The
fewer indicators on the screen, the better my decisions get.
