# YouTube growth research (Nov 2026)

Raw findings for taking a channel from 0 to monetized and then to a product business. Numbers are India-specific where it matters.

## 1. The monetization gates

| Gate | Requirement | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Fan funding tier | 500 subs + (3,000 watch hrs OR 3M Shorts views/90d) | Super Thanks, memberships. No ad revenue. |
| Full YPP (current) | 1,000 subs + (4,000 watch hrs/12mo OR 10M Shorts views/90d) | Ad revenue |
| Full YPP (from Feb 1, 2027) | 1,000 subs + (8,000 watch hrs/365d OR 20M Shorts views/90d) | Ad revenue |

- Shorts watch time NEVER counts toward the watch-hours path. The two paths are separate ladders.
- Channels already in YPP before Feb 2027 are grandfathered — they do not have to requalify.
- Announced Aug 10, 2026. Effective Feb 1, 2027.

**Implication:** a channel starting Nov 2026 will almost certainly not clear either gate before Feb 1. Plan for the 2027 numbers. Do not let the deadline distort the strategy.

## 2. The money math (India, 2026)

- Shorts RPM: **₹5–₹30 per 1,000 views**. 1M Shorts views = ₹5,000–₹30,000.
- Long-form RPM: **₹25–₹150 per 1,000 views**. 1M views = ₹25,000–₹1,50,000.
- Shorts pay from a shared pool, creator keeps 45%. Long-form keeps 55% of net ad revenue.
- Long-form earns **50–250x more per view** than Shorts.

**Implication:** AdSense from Shorts in India is not a business. It is a scoreboard. Hitting ₹1L/month on Shorts alone needs 5–20M views/month.

## 3. Shorts vs long-form: what each is actually for

- Shorts → subscriber conversion: **0.05–0.5%** per view.
- Long-form → subscriber conversion: **2–5%** per view (roughly 10x better).
- Channels that deliberately engineer a Shorts→long-form handoff grow long-form subs ~41% faster than channels running Shorts standalone.
- Shorts subscribers are low-intent — many never watch long-form.

**Shorts = reach. Long-form = trust, watch hours, and money.** A product business needs trust, so long-form is not optional, only deferrable.

## 4. What actually drives a Short

- **50–60% of all drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds.**
- 3-second retention is the hook metric: **below 45% = broken hook, above 55% = working hook.**
- Moving retention from 30% → 55% can increase reach **10–50x**. Reach is a step function, not a slope.
- The algorithm seeds every Short to a small test audience. Weak engagement = distribution stops. There is no recovery.
- "Engaged views" is the metric that matters: watched meaningfully past the opening, liked, commented, shared, or replayed.
- Shorts can be 3 minutes, but **50–60 seconds has the best watch-through (~76%)**.

## 5. What is getting views right now (Nov 2026)

Working formats: mini vlogs / day-in-the-life, food, DIY and fast tutorials, short explainers, listicles, before-and-after transformations, comparisons, surprising facts, storytelling Shorts, trend remixes.

Emerging: non-verbal / POV Shorts, seasonal transformations, podcast clip funnels, Shorts Series and microdramas, AI-assisted animation.

**Faceless formats are now ~38% of new creator monetization ventures.**

## 6. Faceless / AI content: allowed, but the bar moved

- YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (renamed from "repetitious content", July 2025) does NOT ban AI or faceless channels. It judges the finished video, not how it was made.
- What gets demonetized: generic/repetitive content, mass-produced templated uploads, unsatisfying or off-putting content.
- **A wave in Jan 2026 wiped 16 channels** for templated, replicable-at-scale uploads.
- Hard ban: **AI personas presented as human experts on health, legal, finance, or politics.** No exceptions, no matter how good the script.
- Safe: AI used to express a voice a template could not produce — your own characters, narrative, insight, commentary.

**Implication:** automation is allowed in the pipeline, not in the point of view. The thing that cannot be automated is the thing that keeps the channel alive.

## 7. Cold start reality

- Day 1 on a new channel: **10–30 views per Short is normal.** Not broken.
- Under 1,000 subs: 50–500 views in first 48h is the normal band.
- **30–60 days of consistent posting** before the algorithm reliably recommends you.
- By day 30, expect 100–500 views per Short.
- Minimum viable cadence: **5 Shorts/week.** Ideal for fast learning: 1/day for 30 days = 30 clean data points.
- Recommended split once running: 3–5 Shorts/week to grow, 1–2 long-form/week to earn.

## 8. Packaging and niche discipline

- **Title + thumbnail carry ~70% of CTR variance.** Packaging is the product; the video is the delivery.
- Decide title and thumbnail **before recording**, not after. Write to the promise.
- Generate 3–4 thumbnail variations, use YouTube's built-in thumbnail A/B test.
- Titles should pattern-match what is already working in the sub-niche — novelty belongs in the content, not the title structure.
- **One niche, one format, locked for 12 uploads minimum.** Channel-hopping resets the algorithm's model of who to show you to.

## 9. How professional channels are staffed (7 roles)

1. **Producer / creative director** — owns strategy, what gets made and why
2. **Line producer** — owns the shoot
3. **Scriptwriter** — owns the script, usually supervised by the producer
4. **Long-form editor**
5. **Short-form editor** (different skill — pacing, not storytelling)
6. **Thumbnail designer** — owns the whole visual identity, not just thumbnails
7. **QC / ops** — final check and publishing

The creator personally owns **title and thumbnail** in nearly every well-run channel. That decision is never delegated.

Typical stage pipeline: idea capture → title+thumbnail → script → film → edit → package → publish → review.

## 10. The product endgame

- Realistic threshold for a self-serve digital product: **5,000–10,000 engaged followers** (engaged, not raw subs).
- A 10K engaged audience with a working product funnel is in the **$5,000/month** range — which beats AdSense at several hundred thousand views/month.
- Creators who own an email list are **2.7x** more likely to earn higher incomes.
- The pattern that wins: own a list, sell something you make, treat platforms as discovery and not as the payday.

**Implication:** the channel is distribution. The product is the business. Build the email capture from video #1, not after monetization.
