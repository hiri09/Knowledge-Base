# YouTube niche economics (Nov 2026)

Why the niche choice decides the income more than the view count does.

## 1. RPM by niche — a 25x spread

| Niche | RPM (per 1,000 views) |
|---|---|
| Personal finance / investing | $25–$50 |
| Software & tech reviews | $20–$40 |
| Legal & tax education | $15–$45 CPM |
| **Business documentary / case study** | **$5–$14** |
| Business / entrepreneurship (broad) | $4–$9 |
| Tech (general) | $5–$10 |
| **Entertainment, comedy, pranks** | **$1–$3 (lowest tier)** |

At 100K monthly views: finance ≈ $3,000–10,000/mo. Entertainment ≈ $200–500/mo. Same views, same effort.

## 2. Geography is a second multiplier — 10–20x

- US viewer RPM: **$2.20–$6.60** per 1,000. India viewer RPM: **$0.13–$0.68** per 1,000.
- Indian RPM is **8–20x lower** than US for identical content, because Indian advertisers bid lower.
- **Indian creators making English content for US/UK audiences earn $3–$11 RPM — same as Western creators.** Physical location is irrelevant; language, topic and SEO targeting decide the audience.

**The two multipliers stack.** Entertainment + Indian audience is the floor. Business + global English audience is 20–100x that per view.

## 3. Business documentary: the proven faceless format

Established channels: MagnatesMedia, ColdFusion, Company Man, Modern MBA, Logically Answered, Business Casual, Why It Failed, Bankrupt Giants, TheCollapseCo.

- Reported RPM **$5–$14**; monthly AdSense $3,300–$8,400; total revenue $6,000–$18,000.
- Typical format: cinematic 12–17 min investigations, voiceover + graphics, no face.
- **Failure stories consistently outperform success stories on engagement.** MagnatesMedia's "How [Company] Lost Everything" is the template everyone copies.
- Among the highest-revenue faceless niches overall — high watch time plus a business-minded (expensive) audience.

Downside: **this field is crowded and the quality bar is high.** Entry needs a specific wedge, not a general "business stories" channel.

## 4. Copyright: recap vs analysis

- **Content ID cannot evaluate fair use.** It matches footage. Legally sound commentary still gets claimed, and a claim sends the revenue to the claimant.
- A Content ID claim ≠ a strike. Claims take revenue; **3 strikes ends the channel.**
- Strong end of fair use: video essays and analysis that argue *about* the footage.
- Weak end: "recap and compilation channels that retell the movie with its own footage — studios enforce against them."
- There is **no safe duration**. No 10-second or 30-second rule exists.

## 5. Multi-channel: correlated risk, not diversification

- Channels sharing an AdSense ID get investigated together. Documented case: **3 profitable channels built over ~2 years, all terminated from one inauthentic strike** on a shared AdSense ID.
- "Multiple channels running the same template is exactly the mass-produced-templates bucket, just distributed."
- Each channel independently needs 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views/90d (20M after Feb 2027) — roughly 110k views/day sustained, per channel.
- Correct sequence: **one channel to monetization, prove the format, then clone the system** with a distinct angle and separate entity.

## 6. Low-competition niches found (not recommended, logged for completeness)

- Manhwa recaps — $10+ RPM, 5.8x growth, but heavy copyright exposure.
- Trading app tutorials for beginners — high CPM, under 10K channels, but needs real finance credibility.
- Senior health micro-niches — wide open, low reach ceiling.
- Nature/wildlife, meditation/ambience — easy to produce, very low RPM.

**Caveat from the research: low competition often signals weak demand, not opportunity.** Validate demand before treating an empty niche as a gap.

## Conclusion

The subject can stay mass-appeal. The **framing** decides the RPM. "What happened in the movie" is entertainment ($1–3). "Where the money went" is business ($5–14), on the same subject, with a fraction of the copyright exposure.
